## Problem Statement

KeepItLocal Workspace is v1 feature-complete (everything on the finishing slate is
shipped except licensing). The owner's goal for this cycle is NOT launch or revenue —
it is: **make every existing tool the highest class it can possibly be, measured
against the owner as customer #1.** "If the application makes my life much more
comfortable, we achieved what I wanted." Going public is a later, still-open decision;
the app stays private for now.

Two sub-problems the owner named directly:
1. Tools may or may not already be excellent — "maybe they are already, who knows."
   Nobody has audited them against a written bar.
2. The repo contains stale code. Exhaustive usage-tracing is slower than just asking
   the owner — "ask me if I use this or that and I will answer, it's much faster."

## Demand Evidence

Honest record: **one user — the owner.** Daily dogfooding, no external installs, no
demand signals collected from other humans. The owner explicitly rejected the
"distribution is the bottleneck" premise: "I never tried because I think the product
is not finished and in a condition to be made live." This is a deliberate product-first
choice, recorded here so future sessions don't re-litigate it as neglect.

## Status Quo

The owner's own words: without KeepItLocal you need "10 applications + 10 web
applications" — Everything, Win+V, PowerToys, ezgif, online converters — and the app
folds them into one local, no-admin, no-network-for-core-work suite. Search goes past
Everything's scope: file, folder, inside-file, inside-image (OCR), semantic, and fuzzy
(currently edit-distance-1). Note: the search index is Tantivy (BM25) + watcher, NOT
Everything's NTFS/MFT approach — the MFT fast-path was deliberately removed (needs
admin, violates the no-UAC law; decision registry: `KeepItLocal.md` §8).

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The owner **refused to narrow** ("I refuse to narrow" — explicit choice, D4.1) and is
the target user for this cycle. Recorded for the future-public scenario, not for now:

- **Door/suite insight (EUREKA, agreed in session):** pillar #6 "beat the free version
  or it doesn't ship" is secretly a marketing engine — each tool that beats a free web
  tool is a separate acquisition door ("OCR without uploading", "compress with no
  watermark"); the suite is the retention story. You never narrow the product, you
  narrow each door. Thirty tools = thirty landing pages.
- Evidence people pay for the search pillar alone on Windows: X1 Search (~$96/yr),
  Copernic (annual) — both sell content search, which KeepItLocal already does locally.

## Premises (revised with owner, D6)

1. **The measure of success is the owner.** v1 is "brilliantly good" when it measurably
   improves the owner's daily work. Public/profit are parked, not abandoned.
2. **"Better for me" must be measured, not felt.** A sole user unconsciously routes
   around his own product's bugs; deliberate fresh-eyes passes are required or
   "brilliant" quietly becomes "familiar."
3. **"Finished" needs a written definition** — a concrete bar per surface. Otherwise
   "not ready to go live" can never be satisfied, by construction.
4. **Keep the monetization door open at zero cost.** Locked decisions already do this
   (Apache-only deps, licensing pattern ratified 2026-07-23, no GPL contamination).
   Build nothing for it yet, break nothing for it either.

## Approaches Considered

- **A: Friction Ledger** (minimal) — one week of 5-second friction logging via Quick
  Notes, fix the top 10 by frequency × annoyance. Not chosen as the frame, but its
  logging week survives as The Assignment (evidence feed for the audit).
- **B: Definition of Finished** (ideal) — write the per-surface quality bar, clear all
  roadmap `[Verify]` items, measure real numbers on 4–8 GB target hardware.
- **C: Daily-Loop Deepening** (lateral) — pull forward browser-tabs-in-palette and the
  subsequence fuzzy matcher; frecency-driven hiding of unused tools. Deferred: adds
  code while existing tools are unaudited; both features stay queued in v2, with one
  narrow exception defined in step 4 (matcher pull-forward only if the ledger demands it).
- **Chosen: owner's hybrid ("Tool Excellence Audit")** — B's rigor, scoped to existing
  tools only, with the owner in the loop as the liveness oracle instead of exhaustive
  static tracing.

## Recommended Approach — The Tool Excellence Audit

Make what exists excellent before adding anything. Seven steps, in order.
**Sequencing:** steps 1–3 run *during* the friction-ledger week (see The Assignment);
step 4 starts when the ledger closes. **Priority rule:** the ledger's ranking orders
the tools — cluster free-text entries into distinct frictions, score each
`occurrence count × mean annoyance digit`, rank descending; the step-4 sequence below
is the fallback for tools the ledger never touched. Within a tool, finding severity
orders the fixes. **Search always gets the deepest audit depth** (it's the flagship);
its *order* follows the ledger like everything else.
**Severity scale:** P0 = wrong result / data loss / blocks the task · P1 = works but
causes friction or a workaround · P2 = polish.

1. **Inventory.** Enumerate every visible surface from `src/lib/appScreens.ts` +
   tool-pack registry: 3 pillars, 6 core surfaces, ~30 catalog tools. **Plus the
   invisible machinery (outside-voice T5):** Settings, tray / palette-only launch
   behavior, global + per-app hotkeys, the snippet keyboard hook, clipboard capture
   service, the index watcher, reminders scheduling — each gets an inventory row and
   the same rubric treatment as a screen.
2. **Owner triage (one batched pass, ~10 minutes).** For each tool the owner answers:
   *weekly / rarely / never*. Never-used tools are candidates for the existing
   `hidden?` flag ("get used or get hidden" — the natural extension of "beat free or
   get deleted"); the owner confirms each hide in the same triage pass, and hides land
   as normal owner-reviewed commits (same confirmation pattern as step 7). Owner
   answers beat static analysis; this is the fast path he asked for.
3. **Write the excellence rubric (the "definition of finished," premise 3).** Per
   tool: correctness on edge cases · performance on the canonical 8 GB no-GPU bar
   (4 GB best-effort — see Dependencies) ·
   empty/loading/error states · keyboard-first flow · works on first run with zero
   setup · long operations show progress and cancel cleanly · the `KeepItLocal.md` §7
   checklist items (focus-visible, reduced motion). One page, saved as
   `docs/designs/excellence-rubric.md`. **Provisional performance budgets
   (eng-review 3A — revise after first 8 GB VM measurements):** palette open
   ≤ 120 ms · search first results ≤ 250 ms P95 · cold start ≤ 3 s · idle RAM
   ≤ 300 MB.
4. **Audit used tools against the rubric.** Order comes from the priority rule (ledger
   score; owner's gut order as tie-break); the fallback order when the ledger is silent
   on a tool: Search, Clipboard, Voice, Palette, Notes, then catalog packs. **Search
   gets the deepest pass whenever it is audited** — ranking quality, index freshness
   under churn, the `ntpd`→`notepad` fuzzy gap (edit-distance-1 today; the audit
   *documents* the gap — the subsequence matcher stays queued in v2 and is pulled
   forward ONLY if the ledger ranks this gap in the top 10; licensing already settled:
   fzf/MIT clean-room, `KeepItLocal.md` §8), OCR hit quality, semantic on/off behavior.
   *Weekly* tools get the full rubric; *rarely* tools are audited against the reduced
   subset only (correctness + error states). **Search acceptance = the query corpus
   (outside-voice T3):** the owner writes 30–50 real queries with their expected
   top-3 result (filename lookups, content phrases, OCR text known to be in a
   screenshot, fuzzy typos like `ntpd`→`notepad`), harnessed as an automated Rust
   test against the index. Search passes when the corpus passes; the corpus is the
   permanent regression net for every future ranking/index change. **The fuzzy
   matcher's pull-forward trigger becomes corpus evidence** (fuzzy-class corpus
   failures), replacing the ledger-top-10 popularity trigger.
5. **Fix ranked findings — bounded.** At most the **top 3 findings per weekly tool per
   cycle** (exception: any friction in the ledger's overall top 10 is always in scope,
   even past the cap); *rarely* tools get **P0 fixes only**. Worst-first; each cycle is
   time-boxed to **4 weeks from that cycle's ledger close**. Each new cycle opens with
   a fresh one-week friction ledger plus a triage re-run, and its ranking supersedes
   the previous cycle's. Every fix verified per `KeepItLocal.md` §7 gates (svelte-check
   0 errors, Vitest for pure logic, cargo test for Rust). **Regression rule
   (eng-review 2A): every P0/P1 fix ships with a test that reproduces the defect** —
   Vitest for TS pure logic, `cargo test` for Rust; friction that lives purely in UI
   interaction gets a scripted manual verify note in the finding log instead
   (owner accepted the recurrence risk of notes without a re-run ritual —
   outside-voice T4 rejected). **Debt carry-forward + stop rule (outside-voice
   T2):** open P0/P1 findings persist across cycles as a debt list (one section in
   `docs/designs/excellence-rubric.md`) until fixed or owner-waived — a new cycle's
   ranking never erases them. The program completes when a full cycle yields zero
   new P0/P1 findings AND the debt list is empty.
6. **Clear the `[Verify]` backlog for used surfaces.** Real-app runs for: screen
   recorder non-PATH FFmpeg install, media utility extract/compress outputs,
   palette-only launch mode, running-state dots. An item whose tool the triage marks
   *never-used* drops with the tool (re-verified if the tool is ever un-hidden).
7. **Stale-code sweep.** Grep finds *candidates only* (unreferenced commands, orphaned
   components, superseded modules) — Tauri invoke-by-string and runtime-built event
   names can hide references, so string-built call sites get a manual check before
   anything is proposed. The owner confirms each candidate with a yes/no; confirmed
   dead code is deleted. Exception: the Disclosure Firewall is staged-inert by
   design, NOT stale — do not delete (`roadmap.md` §1 warning).

Each audit finding gets logged with severity (P0–P2 scale above) + file:line; fixes
land as small, reviewable commits (owner commits — never auto-commit, `KeepItLocal.md` §7).

## Success Criteria

- Owner-felt: after the audit cycle, the owner can say "this application makes my life
  much more comfortable" about every tool he actually uses — and nothing he uses makes
  him reach for one of the old 10 apps / 10 websites.
- Measurable (assessed at the end of the *final* cycle of the repeating program, not
  necessarily the first 4-week cycle): every *weekly* tool passes the full rubric;
  every *rarely* tool passes correctness + error-states minimum; zero `[Verify]` items
  remain on used surfaces; zero owner-confirmed stale code remains; every ledger
  top-10 friction is verified fixed or explicitly waived by the owner.
- Search specifically: the personal query corpus (30–50 real queries with expected
  top-3 results, outside-voice T3) passes as an automated test.

## Distribution Plan

Deliberately parked — the app stays private this cycle (owner's explicit call).
Already-locked decisions that keep the door open: manual-download updates, online
activation + offline operation with grace window (never online-only), free-tier
feature-gating architecture, Apache-2.0-only ML deps. No CI/CD or store work now.

## Dependencies

- Owner availability for the triage pass and stale-code confirmations (minutes, not
  hours — batched).
- Native runtime env (libvosk/tesseract) for full-app verification builds.
- A RAM-constrained test environment for the rubric's performance checks. **Canonical
  bar: an 8 GB Windows VM.** Tightening the app's own Job-Object caps is an
  approximation only (it limits the process, not system-wide memory pressure); the
  4 GB end of the stated range is best-effort, not verified.
- No new dependencies anticipated; the subsequence fuzzy matcher (only if its ledger
  condition in step 4 fires) sources from fzf (MIT) per the settled `KeepItLocal.md`
  §8 decision.

## The Assignment

**Starting today, run the friction ledger for one week:** every time KeepItLocal makes
you hesitate, wait, guess, or reach for one of the old tools/websites, capture it in
Quick Notes (the hotkey you already built) in ≤5 seconds — what you were doing, what
went wrong, and **one digit 1–3 for how much it annoyed you** (3 = rage).
**P0 carve-out (outside-voice T1):** a data-loss or task-blocking defect found
during the week gets fixed immediately (still logged as its `fx3` entry) — the
freeze applies to P1/P2 friction only, so avoidance never skews the ledger.
**Capture convention (eng-review 1A):** every ledger entry starts with `fx<digit> `
— e.g. `fx3 palette lost my query on tab switch` — so week's-end clustering is one
grep over Quick Notes, and the annoyance digit is never forgotten. Don't fix
anything during the week; the audit's steps 1–3 run in parallel with it. At the end,
that list — ranked by frequency × annoyance — becomes the audit's priority order
(replacing the pre-ledger default in step 4). You are the only user, so
this ledger is the only real usage data that exists. The audit without it is guesswork;
with it, it's evidence.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You already run the hardest product rule I've seen in a solo project: "beat free or
  get deleted." Most founders can't kill their own features; your roadmap shows three
  tools hidden for exactly that reason. That discipline is rare.
- When I pushed you to narrow, you didn't waffle — you said "I refuse to narrow" and
  gave reasons ("you have to use 10 applications + 10 web applications to achieve
  things I give you inside one local application"). Wrong or right, that's conviction
  with reasoning, not compliance.
- "Ask me if I use this or that and I will answer — it's much faster" — you instinctively
  chose the cheapest sufficient oracle over the thorough-looking one. That's senior
  engineering judgment applied to your own attention.
- You called yourself "the first customer and owner all in one." Hold on to the first
  half: the day you stop being a *customer* who complains about friction and become
  only an *owner* who forgives it, the quality bar silently dies.
